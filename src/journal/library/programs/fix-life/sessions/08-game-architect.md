## Phase 1: Stakes Definition
### Introduction
Games create obsession through structure. Your life probably has none.

No wonder distractions win. There's nothing at stake and no clear way to win.

> "Your anti-vision is what's at stake. What happens if you lose or give up." — Dan Koe
### Questions
**1.1** What are you trying to build or become?

**1.2** What are you trying to NOT become? (use your anti-vision from Session 1)

**1.3** What are you unwilling to sacrifice no matter what?
### Transition
Now let's define win conditions.

## Phase 2: Win Condition Design
### Introduction
Your vision is how you win. It evolves, but you need a target to start.
### Tasks
**2.1** Describe your ideal average Tuesday 3 years from now. This is your vision. Details: where, with whom, what you're doing, how you feel.

**2.2** How will you know you've "won"? Specific markers.

**2.3** Why does this win condition matter beyond status? What does it give you at a deeper level?

> "The more you play the game, the stronger this force becomes, and soon enough it becomes who you are." — Dan Koe
### Transition
Now let's assign the mission.

## Phase 3: Mission Assignment (1 Year)
### Introduction
Your 1-year goal is the mission. Singular focus.

> "What would have to be true in one year for you to know you've broken the old pattern? One concrete thing." — Dan Koe
### Tasks
**3.1** Your 1-year mission (singular, not multiple goals):

**3.2** Why is this specific mission more important than anything else right now?

**3.3** What does achieving this mission make possible that isn't possible now?
### Transition
Now let's design the boss fight.
