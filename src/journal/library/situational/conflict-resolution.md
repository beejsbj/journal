Don't take sides or judge the other person, unless they describe being harmed by that person: then their safety comes first, and you don't ask them to see the other side. Otherwise, ask.

## Phase 1: What happened
### Introduction
A conflict looks different once each side's need is on the table. Start with what happened, as plainly as you can.

This is for a conflict between people on fairly even footing. If this person has hurt or frightened you, skip the questions about your part and their side; you don't owe them that, and someone you trust can help.
### Questions
**1.1** Who is the conflict with, and what happened? Keep to what was said and done, before what you made of it.
**1.2** What did you feel at the time, and what do you feel about it now?
### Transition
Under most positions there's a need. Start with yours.

## Phase 2: Your side
**2.1** What did you need that you weren't getting? To be heard, respected, rested, treated fairly, something else?
**2.2** What part did you play in how it went, even a small one?
### Transition
Now the harder chair: theirs.

## Phase 3: Their side
**3.1** Tell it from their side, the way they might, in words they'd recognise. What were they feeling?
**3.2** What might they have needed?
### Transition
Now where the two can meet.

## Phase 4: A way forward
### Introduction
If the first step is a conversation, Conversation prep (/start prep) can help you get ready for it.
### Questions
**4.1** What would a good outcome look like for both of you? What's one clear, doable thing you could ask for?
**4.2** What's your first step, and when: something to say, something to repair, or something to let go?
